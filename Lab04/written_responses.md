# 2) Pick one investment that should be sold. Who should it be sold to, and what is the maximum price it could be sold for? What utility gain is provided to each party at that price?

We chose Investment 1, which is owned by Client A. It has a 60% chance of paying $80,000.

Client A's certainty equivalent is $12,966.61, while Client C's certainty equivalent is $42,286.44. Therefore, Client A should sell it to Client C, and the maximum price is $42,286.44.

At this price, Client A's utility increases from approximately 0.527334 to 0.753568, giving a utility gain of approximately 0.226234. Client C's utility gain is 0 at the maximum price because C is indifferent between buying the investment and keeping the money.

This shows that both clients can benefit from the trade if the price is below the maximum. At the maximum price, the seller benefits, but the buyer is just indifferent.
